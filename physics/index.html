CHAPTER 01

Measurements

A physical quantity is a measured property expressed by a number and unit. SI base units include metre, kilogram, second, ampere, kelvin, mole and candela. Accuracy means closeness to an accepted value; precision means agreement between repeated readings. Dimensional analysis checks equation consistency but cannot determine dimensionless constants.

Core concept summary

A physical quantity is a measured property expressed by a number and unit. SI base units include metre, kilogram, second, ampere, kelvin, mole and candela. Accuracy means closeness to an accepted value; precision means agreement between repeated readings. Dimensional analysis checks equation consistency but cannot determine dimensionless constants.

Solved numerical / example

CHAPTER 02

Force and Motion

Scalars have magnitude only; vectors have magnitude and direction. Newton's first law describes inertia, the second law gives F = ma for constant mass, and the third law describes equal and opposite interaction forces. Momentum is conserved when net external force is zero. Projectile motion separates horizontal and vertical components when air resistance is neglected.

Core concept summary

Scalars have magnitude only; vectors have magnitude and direction. Newton's first law describes inertia, the second law gives F = ma for constant mass, and the third law describes equal and opposite interaction forces. Momentum is conserved when net external force is zero. Projectile motion separates horizontal and vertical components when air resistance is neglected.

CHAPTER 03

Circular and Rotational Motion

Angular velocity is the rate of change of angular displacement. In circular motion acceleration points toward the centre. Centripetal force is provided by real forces such as tension, friction or gravity. Torque is the turning effect of a force; moment of inertia depends on mass distribution and axis.

Core concept summary

Angular velocity is the rate of change of angular displacement. In circular motion acceleration points toward the centre. Centripetal force is provided by real forces such as tension, friction or gravity. Torque is the turning effect of a force; moment of inertia depends on mass distribution and axis.

CHAPTER 04

Work, Energy and Power

Work is force multiplied by displacement component along the force. The work-energy theorem says net work equals change in kinetic energy. Mechanical energy is conserved when only conservative forces do work. Power is the rate of energy transfer.

Core concept summary

Work is force multiplied by displacement component along the force. The work-energy theorem says net work equals change in kinetic energy. Mechanical energy is conserved when only conservative forces do work. Power is the rate of energy transfer.

Solved numerical / example

Important formulas

K = ½mv²
Near Earth: U = mgh
Spring energy = ½kx²
P = W/Δt; P = Fv when parallel

CHAPTER 05

Stress is force per area and strain is fractional deformation. Fluid pressure increases with depth. For steady incompressible flow, the volume flow rate remains constant. Bernoulli's principle relates pressure, speed and height for ideal, steady, non-viscous flow.

CHAPTER 06

Heat and Thermodynamics

Heat is energy transferred because of a temperature difference; temperature describes thermal state. Internal energy is microscopic kinetic and potential energy. The first law, with W as work done by the system, is ΔU = Q − W. An ideal gas obeys PV = nRT with temperature in kelvin.

Core concept summary

Heat is energy transferred because of a temperature difference; temperature describes thermal state. Internal energy is microscopic kinetic and potential energy. The first law, with W as work done by the system, is ΔU = Q − W. An ideal gas obeys PV = nRT with temperature in kelvin.

Solved numerical / example

Important formulas

Periodic motion repeats after a fixed period. In simple harmonic motion, acceleration is proportional to displacement and directed toward equilibrium. Waves transfer energy; wave speed is frequency multiplied by wavelength. Resonance occurs when driving frequency approaches natural frequency.

CHAPTER 08

Physical Optics and Gravitational Waves

Coherent sources have the same frequency and constant phase difference. Constructive interference occurs when waves arrive in phase; destructive interference when out of phase. Diffraction is spreading around obstacles or apertures. Polarization indicates transverse-wave behaviour. Gravitational waves are ripples in spacetime produced by accelerating massive systems.

Core concept summary

Coherent sources have the same frequency and constant phase difference. Constructive interference occurs when waves arrive in phase; destructive interference when out of phase. Diffraction is spreading around obstacles or apertures. Polarization indicates transverse-wave behaviour. Gravitational waves are ripples in spacetime produced by accelerating massive systems.

CHAPTER 09

Electrostatics and Current Electricity

Like charges repel and unlike charges attract. Coulomb's law gives force between point charges. Electric field is force per unit positive test charge; potential difference is work per unit charge. Current is charge flow per second. For ohmic conductors under constant conditions V=IR. In series current is shared; in parallel voltage is shared.

Core concept summary

Like charges repel and unlike charges attract. Coulomb's law gives force between point charges. Electric field is force per unit positive test charge; potential difference is work per unit charge. Current is charge flow per second. For ohmic conductors under constant conditions V=IR. In series current is shared; in parallel voltage is shared.

CHAPTER 11

Special Theory of Relativity

The laws of physics are the same in all inertial frames and the speed of light in vacuum is constant for all inertial observers. Proper time is measured in the frame where events occur at the same place. Relativistic effects become important near light speed. Rest energy relates mass and energy.

Core concept summary

The laws of physics are the same in all inertial frames and the speed of light in vacuum is constant for all inertial observers. Proper time is measured in the frame where events occur at the same place. Relativistic effects become important near light speed. Rest energy relates mass and energy.

Solved numerical / example

For m=2 kg and c=3×10⁸ m/s: E=mc²=2×(3×10⁸)²=1.8×10¹⁷ J.

Method: list given values, select a suitable formula, substitute values, calculate and include the correct unit.

Important formulas

γ = 1/√(1 − v²/c²)
Time dilation: Δt = γΔt₀
Length contraction: L = L₀/γ
Rest energy: E₀ = mc²

CHAPTER 12

Nuclear and Particle Physics

The nucleus contains protons and neutrons. Atomic number is proton number; mass number is nucleon number. Mass defect corresponds to binding energy. Radioactive decay is spontaneous and random. Half-life is the time for undecayed nuclei to halve. Fission splits heavy nuclei; fusion joins light nuclei.

Core concept summary

The nucleus contains protons and neutrons. Atomic number is proton number; mass number is nucleon number. Mass defect corresponds to binding energy. Radioactive decay is spontaneous and random. Half-life is the time for undecayed nuclei to halve. Fission splits heavy nuclei; fusion joins light nuclei.

Solved numerical / example
